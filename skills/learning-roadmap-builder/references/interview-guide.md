# Interview Guide

Ask in the user's language. Use `ask_question` (recommended option first, batch up to 4 related questions per call). Skip anything already answered. Aim for **2–3 rounds** before drafting the blueprint.

## Round 1 — Goal, Outcome & Background

1. **Concrete outcome ("Definition of Done")**: What do you want to be able to *do* or *achieve* by the end?
   - Pass a specific exam / certification / target score
   - Apply directly at work / switch career
   - Build a concrete project / portfolio / deliverable
   - Practical everyday fluency / personal enrichment
2. **Timeline / Deadline**: Fixed exam or launch date · 1–2 months · 3 months · 6+ months · Flexible (recommend for me).
3. **Your background & transfer anchors**: What is your current role/field, and what adjacent skills or hobbies do you already know well? *(Adapt options to the topic — never assume IT. E.g. for finance: business/accounting/engineering/student; for language: native language + other languages known; for design: marketing/dev/art/beginner.)*
4. **Motivation & context**: Where will you apply this? *(Used to personalize examples, case studies, and practice scenarios — e.g. "investing my own salary", "presenting to executives", "traveling to Japan", "building a SaaS".)*

## Round 2 — Verified Level & Diagnostic Probes

5. **Self-rated starting point**: Complete beginner · Know basic concepts · Can do basics with guidance · Intermediate looking to level up / fill gaps.
6. **Diagnostic probes (2–4 tailored questions)**: Write concrete, domain-specific multiple-choice questions spanning **easy → medium → hard**, each including an `"I don't know / Chưa biết"` option.
   - *Technical (SQL)*: `WHERE` vs `HAVING`, `INNER` vs `LEFT JOIN`, index trade-offs.
   - *Language (IELTS)*: identifying thesis/cohesion flaws, choosing register, grammar nuance.
   - *Finance*: Revenue vs Cash Flow, P/E vs EV/EBITDA, diversification vs hedging.
   - *Design (UX)*: Wireframe vs Prototype, heuristic evaluation, quantitative vs qualitative research.
   - *Soft skill (Negotiation)*: BATNA meaning, anchoring vs concession, positional vs interest-based bargaining.
   - **Calibration rule**: ≥75% correct → compress/skip foundations; 40–75% → standard pace; <40% → add prerequisite ramp-up. If probes contradict self-rating, trust the probes and explain gently.
7. **Prior practice exposure**: Never tried · Read/watched tutorials only · Practiced occasionally · Used in real situations.

## Round 3 — Rhythm, Style, Constraints & Risks

8. **Weekly time & session shape** *(critical for practice design)*:
   - Hours/week: 2–3h · 4–6h · 7–10h · 10+h.
   - Session pattern: Short daily micro-sessions (20–30 min) · 3–4 weekday evenings (1h) · Weekend deep blocks (2–3h) · Mixed.
9. **Learning style & theory:practice ratio**:
   - Ratio: Practice-heavy (30:70) · Balanced (50:50) · Concept-first (70:30).
   - Preferred formats (multi-select): Reading/structured notes · Diagrams & visual models · Video/audio · Writing/case analysis · Speaking/roleplay · Hands-on building/drills.
10. **Materials, tools & constraints**:
    - Language of study materials: Native language · English · Mixed (native explanations + original domain terms).
    - Budget & environment: Free-only vs paid courses/books/tools; available tools/equipment/space (laptop, instrument, software, gym, study partner, etc.).
11. **Weak spots & drop-off risks**: Any sub-topics you struggle with, past failed attempts, or what usually makes you quit? *(e.g. "too much theory without practice", "busy weeks break my habit", "no one to give feedback", "forgetting what I learned last week".)*
